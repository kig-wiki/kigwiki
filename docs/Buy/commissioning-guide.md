# How to Order a Kigurumi Mask

Most Kigurumi Masks are commissioned rather than bought from premade stock.  
A good order starts with comparing real examples, choosing a maker whose style fits the character, and agreeing on size, features, as well as verifying the price, and timing before paying.

Use the [Kigurumi Mask maker directory](./makers.md) to find options, then check each maker's current ordering instructions.

## 1. Research the maker

A maker's portfolio is the best starting point, but do not rely only on polished announcement photos. Compare:

- several recent masks, ideally including characters with features similar to yours
- customer photos taken in different lighting and from different angles
- notably mind the hair, face shape, eye design and mouth expression work

[KiguDB](https://kigudb.info/) is an example of a site that can help you cross-reference masks and compare how different handle different styles of character. Price alone does not establish quality, and one excellent or poor example does not describe every commission.

Especially if you are looking at a "pre-made" mask, we highly recommend checking out the [Kigurumi Mask seller red flags](./seller-red-flags.md) page to help you avoid any potential pitfalls.

## 2. Prepare character references

Reference art does not need to be a single formal character sheet, but it should clearly show the result you want.  
Many makers will take the reference art you provide at face value and attempt to make it as close as possible, rather than an interpretation of the character.

Call out specific priorities such as:

- Eye shape and iris design
- Mouth expression
- Skin tone and any makeup if applicable
- Hair color, length, and any particular styling
- Side/back views of the character if the design isn't obvious from the main reference(s)
- Accessories, glasses, horns, ears, or removable parts
- Details the maker may otherwise need to interpret

If multiple images are provided and details conflict, be clear about which parts and sections should take priority, although a single cohesive reference is preferred.  
Commissioning an artist for a reference sheet can strongly help, but is not always required. If it's an existing character, there may be official reference art available that can provide a strong base for the commission.

## 3. Confirm size and fit

Follow the maker's measurement method exactly. Makers may ask for head circumference, head height, shoulder size, your weight, height, or other dimensions.

Ask which shell sizes or custom-sizing options are available. A mask can fit your head and still look out of proportion with your body or character.  
For example a larger shell may suit a taller performer or a character with stylized proportions such as chibi/loli proportions, while an oversized shell can look unbalanced on a smaller frame. Discuss the intended look with the maker to help make the best choice.

## 4. Choose features

Available options vary, but may include:

- a "full shell" or partial back
- interchangeable faceplates
- removable wig components or hard hair
- mesh, lens, follow-me, or illuminated eyes
- fans or other ventilation
- magnetic or detachable accessories like cat ears

## 5. Ask about price and timing

Makers will typically provide a quote for the mask, if it doesn't include shipping estimates, it's a good idea to ask.

Complicated hair, multiple colors, accessories, electronics, extra faceplates, and unusual sizes can all often affect price, although how much so varies by maker.

Clarify:

- the deposit and payment schedule
- accepted payment methods and currency
- whether shipping, customs, duties, or taxes are included in their quote
- when work is expected to start
- the estimated production window

A backlog and a production window are different. A maker may begin several months after the order and then take some time to build and finish it. Treat dates as estimates unless the maker explicitly guarantees them.  
If theres an event you really need the mask for, mention it to the maker and see if they can accommodate.

## 6. Review the order before paying

Before payment, make sure both you and the maker agree on:

- the character and reference set
- dimensions and intended proportions
- expression, hair, eyes, skin tone, and accessories
- included features and removable parts
- total price, payment schedule, and shipping destination
- expected milestones and approval points, most makers will usually contact after the initial 3D model is made if you have any changes or adjustments to make, and again towards the end of the production process to confirm the final details.

Keep copies of the quote, references, measurements, and messages. Use a payment method with appropriate buyer protection, and understand its deadlines and exclusions.

Once the mask arrives, inspect it and contact the maker if it was damaged in transit or differs materially from the approved order.  
For fitting adjustments, see [Kigurumi Mask care and padding](../Care/mask-care.md), as masks often require some level of personalization for the padding to be perfectly fitted.
